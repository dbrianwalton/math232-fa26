Derivatives of the Trigonometric Functions.

With \(x\) in radians, at every \(x\) where the function is defined,
\begin{align*} \frac{d}{dx}\sin(x) \amp = \cos(x) \amp \frac{d}{dx}\cos(x) \amp = -\sin(x) \\ \frac{d}{dx}\tan(x) \amp = \sec^2(x) \amp \frac{d}{dx}\cot(x) \amp = -\csc^2(x) \\ \frac{d}{dx}\sec(x) \amp = \sec(x)\tan(x) \amp \frac{d}{dx}\csc(x) \amp = -\csc(x)\cot(x)\text{.} \end{align*}
The derivatives of cotangent and cosecant follow from the quotient rule in the same way as tangent and secant. Notice that the derivative of each “co” function is negative.
