Worksheet Exercise 1. Solving for an Unknown Exponent.

Recall from Definition 58 that \(e^u=N\) means exactly that \(\ln(N)=u\text{,}\) and that \(\ln(b^u)=u\ln(b)\text{.}\) Because \(\ln\) is one-to-one, applying it to both sides of an equation never creates or loses a solution, as long as both sides are positive.
🔗

(a)

Solve \(e^{3x-1}=5\) for \(x\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(b)

Solve \(4e^{-x/2}+1=13\) for \(x\text{.}\) What must you do before applying \(\ln\text{,}\) and why?
🔗
🔗

(c)

Solve \(2^x=10\) for \(x\) by applying \(\ln\) to both sides. Which property of the logarithm moves the unknown out of the exponent?
🔗
🔗

(d)

Solve \(5\cdot 3^{2t}=40\) for \(t\text{.}\) Write your exact answer using only \(\ln(2)\) and \(\ln(3)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(e)

Explain why \(e^x=-2\) has no solution. What does this say about the range of \(e^x\text{?}\)
🔗
🔗

(f)

A student solves \(3+2^x=11\) by “taking the logarithm of both sides” and writing \(\ln(3)+x\ln(2)=\ln(11)\text{.}\) Explain what went wrong. Then solve the equation correctly.
